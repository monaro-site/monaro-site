Queensland Raceway, and the tracks that come and go

Ipswich has been dropped from the 2027 calendar, two years after it came back. Where the Paperclip came from, who won there, and what it would take to return.

By Matt · 7 min read · Published 10 November 2026 · Last checked 4 November 2026

Queensland Raceway won't be on the 2027 Supercars calendar. Speedcafe broke the news on 4 November, and the official 14-round calendar confirmed it hours later: the only Queensland rounds left are the Townsville and Gold Coast street races. Phillip Island is back as a Finals round on a Victorian Government deal, Sandown moves to late August as the Sprint Cup finale (Ipswich's old job), and Ipswich is the track that loses its place.

It's only two years since the Paperclip came back after five seasons away. Crowds were strong both times, and this August it was where Broc Feeney won the Sprint Cup and Matt Payne's engine let go. So for this Teardown I've stripped QR right down: where it came from, who won there, and then the bigger question. Tracks come and go from this calendar all the time. Which ones have made it back, and what would it take for Ipswich to do it again?

From Lakeside to the Paperclip

For 35 years, Queensland's touring car round was at Lakeside, north of Brisbane. It was on the calendar from 1964 to 1998, it's where Dick Johnson clinched his first title against Peter Brock in 1981, and twice (1989 and 1996) the lake next door flooded it and an event had to be postponed. By the late 1990s the series wanted something bigger.

That something was Queensland Raceway, built on flat land at Willowbank, just outside Ipswich and about 40 minutes west of Brisbane. It opened in 1999 and went straight onto the calendar, replacing Lakeside. At 3.1 km it is short and simple: long straights joined by tight corners, which is why everyone calls it the Paperclip. It's hard on brakes and rewards a car that stays calm under braking, and the long straights give you a proper go at a pass.

In its first two years Ipswich hosted two rounds a season: a sprint round and the Queensland 500, a 500 km endurance race that ran in the weeks before Bathurst. The 500 ran from 1999 to 2002. In 2001 and 2002 it was the only Supercars race at QR, before the enduro went back to Sandown in 2003 and Ipswich got its sprint round back.

From then on it was a fixture every winter, usually in July, until 2019. Long-time owner John Tetley ran it for most of that time. Tony Quinn, the Kiwi businessman who also owns Hampton Downs, Highlands and Taupō, tried to buy it once and saw the deal fall through at the last minute. He finally took it over in late 2021, after Supercars had already left.

The five-year gap

Ipswich missed the 2020 calendar as Supercars consolidated its schedule (and then COVID tore up the season anyway). It was expected back in 2021. It didn't come back for five years.

Quinn spent the gap rebuilding it: new pit buildings, new catch fencing, and a resurface. When Supercars announced its return for 2025, CEO Shane Howard called it a "truly revitalised" venue. The return lifted the calendar to 13 events, and Ipswich was handed the Sprint Cup finale, with a guaranteed Finals spot going to the driver on top afterwards.

Who won at Ipswich

The first ever Supercars race at QR, in July 1999, went to Russell Ingall in the Perkins Commodore, with John Bowe on pole. But the round went to a young Garth Tander, who finished second, third and second for Garry Rogers Motorsport in an old Holden Racing Team car. It was his first round win in the championship, by two points from Craig Lowndes.

Lowndes is the King of the Paperclip. He raced at every one of QR's Supercars rounds and won 12 races there, more than anyone else. Jamie Whincup and Garth Tander are next with five each.

A few bits I love from the record book:

  • Closest finish: 0.2423 seconds, when James Courtney beat Mark Winterbottom in 2008 for his first ever Supercars win.
  • Pole means little here. Going into 2018, the pole-sitter had won only two of the previous 12 races. Payne found that out this year, starting from pole twice and leaving with nothing.
  • The Brown double: Will Brown won at Ipswich in August 2025, and didn't win again until he went back there a year later.
  • Two Sprint Cups decided here, both won by Feeney.

The tracks that came and went

Ipswich isn't the first track to lose its place, and it won't be the last. The calendar has always moved with the money, the cars and the cities.

Gone for good

  • Surfers Paradise Raceway (1969 to 1987). Owner Keith Williams sold it to fund his Hamilton Island resort, and the new owners closed it. Peter Brock won six of his 13 starts there.
  • Amaroo Park, Sydney (on and off until 1994). At 1.94 km it was simply too small once the five-litre V8s arrived in 1993. It's housing now.
  • Lakeside (1964 to 1998). Replaced by QR. It still runs club racing, but has had its own fights over noise.
  • Calder Park, Melbourne (until 2001). Home of the Thunderdome, and of Lowndes' huge rollover in 1999.
  • Oran Park, Sydney (1971 to 2008). Swallowed by housing in Sydney's south-west. It's where Lowndes did the first champion's burnout in 1996 and where Whincup won his first title in 2008. Mark Skaife won 15 races there.
  • The overseas ones: Shanghai, Bahrain, Abu Dhabi and Austin all had a go between 2005 and 2013, and none lasted.

The ones that came back

  • Amaroo Park was on the calendar in the 1970s, dropped, then came back in 1985 for another decade.
  • Calder Park was off for several seasons over a sponsorship deal, then returned in 1996.
  • Pukekohe in New Zealand ran from 2001 to 2007, lost out to the Hamilton street race, then came back in 2013 once Auckland's council put up money for upgrades. It stayed until 2022, when the track closed.
  • Queensland Raceway came back in 2025 after five years, once a new owner had rebuilt it.
  • Phillip Island comes back in 2027 after eight years away (it last hosted Supercars in 2019), with five years of Victorian Government money behind it, as a Finals round.

And some are newer arrivals still finding their feet: The Bend (from 2018), Taupō (from 2024) and Ruapuna in Christchurch (from 2026).

How Ipswich could come back

Look at the tracks that made it back and the same few things keep turning up: somebody pays, the venue is up to standard, and there's a gap in the calendar it fits. QR already ticks the middle box. Quinn has spent the money, and he's not shy about pointing out that other venues haven't (he called Sandown "a disgrace" this week). So it comes down to money and a slot.

  1. Government money. This is the big one. Gold Coast and Townsville get Queensland Government backing; Ipswich didn't. Ipswich's council did chip in, but Quinn says it "was just not enough". Phillip Island is back because Victoria needed an event after losing MotoGP. If Queensland or a bigger council deal stepped up, Ipswich would be back in the conversation.
  2. A 15th round. The teams' charter only commits them to 12 events, so the two extra rounds that make up 2027's 14 already have to be paid for. Bringing Ipswich back without dropping someone else would mean a 15th round, and another payment to the teams. If the teams and the series' owners settle that argument, a bigger calendar has room for the Paperclip again.
  3. Sandown slipping. If Sandown's facilities keep ageing and nobody in Victoria pays to fix them, QR is the ready-made replacement for a Sprint Cup finale. It's done the job twice.
  4. Something different. QR has floodlights for club night racing. A Supercars night race, or bringing back a Queensland 500 enduro in winter, would give it a reason to exist on the calendar that a normal sprint round doesn't.
  5. The fans. Crowds were strong in 2025 and 2026. Quinn's point is that south-east Queensland fans who don't want to pay Gold Coast prices have just lost their race. If those fans keep turning up and making noise, that helps.

My guess? Ipswich isn't gone forever. Pukekohe came back after five years, Calder after several, Phillip Island after eight. QR has a keen owner, a rebuilt venue and two good years to point to. What it needs is someone to write the cheque.
