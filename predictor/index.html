Who wins the 2026 title?

There's been so much movement this season that I wanted to see who really has the edge. So I ran the rest of the year 60,000 times: Gold Coast, Sandown and the Adelaide Grand Final, with all 24 cars in every race. Each run weighs this year's results, last year's Finals, the type of track, the weather and the grid changes. Matt Payne wins almost half of them, and Will Brown is the one closing in.

Percentages are the share of simulated seasons each driver won. They're chances, not a promise. This is motorsport, after all.

How I built it

  1. Each driver gets a rating from the factors above, weighted as shown. Will Brown's results before his restrictor was fixed are lifted by half the gap between his form before and after the fix, because the car, not the driver, was holding him back. Each pole adds half a point, and drivers in a team that changed manufacturer this year (Triple Eight to Ford, Walkinshaw and Brad Jones to Toyota) have last year's results counted at half weight, because they were in a different car.
  2. Every race, each car's result is its rating plus a random swing. The size of the swing comes from how much results really varied this year. On top of that, each driver gets one swing for the whole weekend, because a car that is off the pace on Saturday usually still is on Sunday.
  3. Each car can also retire. The chance comes from its own record this year, pulled towards the field average so one bad weekend doesn't dominate. Mostert (4 DNFs) and Wood (3) carry the most risk of the ten.
  4. Each race has a chance of a wet race. For the Gold Coast it comes from the Bureau of Meteorology forecast: 55% for Saturday and 70% for Sunday. Sandown (25%) and Adelaide (10%) use climate averages until their forecasts are out. In a wet race the order leans 40% towards wet-weather form, with bigger swings and more retirements.
  5. Qualifying counts too. Race 31's result leans 40% on the qualifying order, because passing is hard on a street track, and Race 32 takes 15% from it as a sign of pace. Drivers outside the top ten are slotted in by rating until the full order is published.
  6. Points use the 2025 Finals scales: 150 for a 250 km win and 138 for 2nd, then 50 and 125 for the Adelaide races. Bonuses of 150, 120, 96 and 78 go to the leaders into each round, and the cuts happen at 7 and 4.

What it can't see

A model is only as good as what goes into it, and there's plenty it can't know.

  • Team-mates racing each other. Grove (Payne and Allen), Triple Eight (Feeney and Brown) and Walkinshaw TWG (Mostert and Wood) each have two cars in the Finals, and team tactics could help one of them.
  • 2025 results at these tracks are mostly known. The Gold Coast top ten from both races and most of race 1 are in. Kostecki, De Pasquale and Le Brocq finished outside the top ten on the Sunday, so those places are estimated at 17th, and Kostecki's Saturday at 21st. Sandown and Adelaide are still partial.
  • Penalties, safety cars and strategy calls. They show up only as random swings.
  • The 2026 points scale for the Finals races hasn't been published in full. I've used the 2025 scale. If 2026 differs, the odds will move a little but the order shouldn't change much.
